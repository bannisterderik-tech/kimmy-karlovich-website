Septic Systems 101 for Douglas County Buyers & Sellers

Standard, sand filter, and ATT systems; inspections, site evaluations, repairs, and what septic really costs in rural Oregon.

How rural sewage actually works

Outside city limits, your property treats its own wastewater: a septic tank settles solids, and a drainfield disperses effluent into soil that finishes the treatment. When soil, slope, or groundwater don't cooperate, engineered systems — sand filters, ATTs (Alternative Treatment Technologies) — do the extra work at extra cost. Which system a property has, and whether it's healthy, is a four-to-five-figure question.

Buying a home with an existing system

Order a septic inspection with tank pumping — not just a "dye test." The inspector evaluates tank condition, baffles, distribution box, and drainfield performance. Ask the county for permit records: an unpermitted or undersized system can complicate lending and insurance, and repairs belong in negotiation, not in your first year of ownership.

Buying bare land: the site evaluation

Douglas County Environmental Health performs site evaluations — test pits dug to read soil depth, texture, and seasonal water table — and issues approval for a system type and location. No approval, no house. Kimmy writes land offers contingent on septic feasibility as standard practice, protecting your earnest money while the county does its work.

What systems cost in 2026

Ballpark for Douglas County: conventional gravity systems $15,000–$25,000; pressure distribution $20,000–$30,000; sand filter and ATT systems $30,000–$50,000+. Repairs range from a $2,500 tank/baffle fix to full drainfield replacement approaching new-system cost. These numbers are why septic diligence is never optional.

Selling? Get ahead of it

Pumping and inspecting before listing turns septic from a buyer's fear into a seller's selling point. A documented, healthy system removes the biggest rural-transaction wildcard — and keeps your sale from renegotiating itself in week three.

FAQ

Questions, answered straight

How often should a septic tank be pumped?

Every 3–5 years for a typical household. At sale, pumping plus inspection is the standard — and in Kimmy's transactions, the expectation.

Can any parcel get septic approval?

No. Shallow soil, hardpan, high groundwater, or steep slopes can make a parcel unapprovable — meaning no dwelling, ever, regardless of zoning. This is exactly what a feasibility contingency exists to discover safely.

What is an ATT system?

Alternative Treatment Technology — an engineered system producing cleaner effluent for difficult sites. They work well but cost more upfront, require annual maintenance contracts, and use electricity. Factor the operating cost into ownership.
