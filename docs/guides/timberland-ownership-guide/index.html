Owning Timberland in Oregon: Taxes, Harvests, and Long-Term Value

Forest deferral, the Oregon Forest Practices Act, harvest planning, reforestation, and how timber builds wealth on its own schedule.

Why Douglas fir ground is a savings account

Site-class Douglas County ground grows merchantable Douglas fir on a 35–50 year rotation, growing value while you hunt it, camp it, and watch it. Small tracts (20–160 acres) let regular buyers hold a real timber asset — one of the few investments you can also picnic on.

Forestland deferral keeps taxes tiny

Oregon's forestland special assessment taxes qualifying timber ground at forest-use value — often a small fraction of market-value taxation. Requirements are modest (adequate stocking of merchantable species); disqualification claws back taxes, so keep the ground stocked and the paperwork straight.

Harvesting: the rules of the road

The Oregon Forest Practices Act governs private harvests: notification before logging, stream buffers, wildlife protections, road standards, and mandatory reforestation (typically within two planting seasons, achieving free-to-grow stocking). Hire a reputable consulting forester for anything beyond firewood — they routinely pay for themselves in log marketing alone.

Buying timberland well

Value = land + standing timber + growth potential. A recent clearcut is cheap but decades from income; a 35-year-old stand is a coiled spring. Cruise before you buy, verify replant compliance from prior harvests, confirm legal access for log trucks, and check the dwelling question if a cabin matters to you.

FAQ

Questions, answered straight

How much is an acre of timber worth?

Bare cutover ground and mature-timber ground can differ by $5,000–$15,000+ per acre in this county. Only a cruise answers it for a specific parcel — never buy or sell significant timber on a listing-price guess.

What is severance or harvest tax in Oregon?

Oregon levies a modest per-MBF harvest tax at cutting (the old severance tax structure was replaced). Your forester builds all taxes and fees into net-revenue projections before you commit to a harvest.

Can I live on my timberland?

Only if the parcel qualifies for a dwelling under forest-zone tests — many don't. Verify with Douglas County Planning before buying if a home or cabin is part of the plan.
