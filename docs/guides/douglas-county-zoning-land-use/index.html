Douglas County Zoning & Land Use: FF, EFU, TR and What They Mean

A plain-English tour of rural zoning — what each zone allows, dwelling tests, divisions, home businesses, and how to verify before buying.

Why Oregon zoning is different

Oregon's statewide land-use system (since 1973) deliberately protects farm and forest ground from development. It's why the Umpqua Valley still looks like the Umpqua Valley — and why a rural parcel's zone matters more than its beauty. The zone determines whether you can build a home, add a second dwelling, divide land, or run a business. Never assume; always verify.

The zones you'll actually meet

FF (Farm Forest) — the county's flexible rural workhorse; dwellings generally allowed on legal lots. EFU (Exclusive Farm Use) — serious farm ground; new dwellings must qualify (farm income tests, relative farm help, etc.). TR (Timberland Resource) — commercial forest; dwellings only via narrow tests like the template dwelling. AG/RR-type rural residential pockets — the simplest building scenarios. Every parcel's zone is public record, and Kimmy checks it before the first showing.

The magic words: "legal lot of record"

A parcel must be legally created to be buildable — some old tax lots were never lawfully divided and carry no development rights until cured. A legal-lot determination from the county resolves it definitively. On any parcel with complicated history, this check comes first.

Dwelling tests, briefly

Forest template dwellings look at how many homes and parcels existed nearby in a defined template area; EFU dwellings look at farm income or other qualifying paths; replacement dwellings let you rebuild where a lawful home existed. Each is technical, each is checkable, and each can swing a parcel's value by six figures.

Home businesses, ADUs, and the fine print

Home occupations, farm stands, wineries, and guest quarters each have rural allowances with conditions. Some dreams (event venue, RV park, second rental home) need conditional use permits or simply aren't achievable. Bring the whole plan to the diligence phase — Kimmy would rather kill a bad fit in week one than watch you buy a plan the county will deny.

FAQ

Questions, answered straight

Can I build a house on EFU land?

Only through qualifying paths — commonly a farm-income test, a replacement dwelling, or specific statutory routes. Plenty of EFU parcels will never qualify. It's the first thing to verify on farm-zoned ground.

Can I divide my rural parcel?

Minimum parcel sizes are large in resource zones (often 80+ acres in EFU/TR), making divisions rare. FF and rural residential zones offer more room. Division potential is a value question worth a planning-counter conversation.

What is a template dwelling?

Oregon's test for building one home on forest-zoned land, based on the pattern of existing parcels and dwellings within a defined "template" around the property. Some parcels pass, most don't — and passing dramatically raises value.
