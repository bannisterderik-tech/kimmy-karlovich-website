Preparing Your Rural Home for Sale: The 90-Day Checklist

Septic, well tests, woodstove certs, cleanup priorities, and staging acreage — the pre-listing playbook that prevents renegotiation.

90 days out: kill the surprises

Pump and inspect the septic. Test the well (flow + quality). Certify or replace the woodstove (uncertified stoves must be removed at sale in Oregon). Gather well logs, septic records, water right papers, and outbuilding permits. Every surprise you eliminate now is a renegotiation that never happens in escrow — this is where sellers protect their price.

60 days out: the land is the product

Mow everything mowable. Beat back the blackberries. Fix sagging gates and the worst fence runs. Gravel the potholes in the driveway. Clear the junk pile behind the barn (buyers see hauling costs, not history). Rural buyers are buying the feeling of a cared-for place — and that feeling is built with a brush hog, not a paintbrush.

30 days out: house and outbuildings

Standard house prep applies (declutter, deep clean, touch-up paint, lightbulbs) — then extend it to the shop and barn: swept floors, organized walls, working lights. An organized 30x40 shop sells trucks-and-tools buyers on the spot. Kimmy walks every listing personally and builds the punch list with you, prioritized by return.

Listing week: timing and presentation

Golden-hour photography, drone coverage of the full parcel, mapped boundaries in the marketing, and every diligence document ready to hand buyers on day one. Documented properties sell faster and hold price through inspections — because confident buyers don't nickel-and-dime.

FAQ

Questions, answered straight

Should I test my own well and septic before listing?

Yes — unequivocally. Pre-listing results either become marketing assets or give you time to fix issues on your schedule and budget, rather than under transaction deadline pressure at inspection-repair prices.

What about the woodstove rule?

Oregon law requires removal and destruction of uncertified woodstoves upon home sale. Check the stove's certification label now; a compliant replacement installed calmly beats a scramble in escrow.

Do I need to paint the barn?

Rarely for value — but tidy, functional, and swept outperforms fresh paint. Spend on mowing, fencing, gravel, and cleanup first; those read as "cared-for" from the first drive-in.
